Frequently asked questions

What does IAEyes do?

IAEyes is a small utility for breaks during work. A break lasts a minute: convex and concave lenses flip and travel the whole screen, and after 5–20 minutes at a level, a short challenge at the end of a break lets you move up. There are 100 levels, with a new change every 10. You can also look away for 20 seconds or set timed breaks. Records and notes stay on your device by default.

What is free, and what does Full Journey unlock?

The app is free to download, and levels 1–10 are free. A single non-consumable Apple in-app purchase permanently unlocks levels 11–100 and custom practice. There is no subscription. The US base price is USD 1.99; your local price appears before purchase. Buying does not skip the active practice time or challenge required at each level.

How do I restore a purchase on another device?

Use the same Apple Account, open Unlock below the progress bar, and choose Restore purchases. The purchase works on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Restoring does not enable data sync. In versions with iCloud controls, you can separately opt into journey and history sync using the same iCloud account; it may differ from your App Store account. Apple handles payment and refund requests; see Apple purchase support.

Do the ±D values represent real lens prescriptions?

They are virtual image settings that change lens appearance and visual effects, not prescription values. Screen zooming, concave and convex appearances, and changes in clarity do not change your actual viewing distance or equal the optical effects of physical lenses.

Too much movement, or need a pause?

You can pause or stop, choose a gentler pace and smaller changes, or enable reduced motion. Choose what feels comfortable without aiming for a number of flips. Stop if you feel uncomfortable.

Are records uploaded? Do I need to log in?

No separate app account is needed. Data is local by default. TestFlight 1.0.0 build 18 has no sync controls. In later versions offering iCloud, journey and detailed history have separate switches, both off initially. Enabling one uploads its existing and future data to your private iCloud, not a developer server. Clarity checks, starting size, camera frames and distance settings stay local. Turn a category off to stop future sync on this device, or use its confirmed cloud-delete action to remove its iCloud payload while retaining local copies. Other devices stop that category at their next sync; re-enabling can upload local data again. Your device’s backup or migration settings may affect saved copies; see the Privacy Policy.

How do I clear data on iPhone or iPad?

Go to Settings → General → iPhone / iPad Storage → IAEyes and choose “Delete App.” “Offload App” keeps documents and data. If a system backup still contains older copies, manage those separately in backup settings.

Does deleting the Mac app also delete its data?

Not necessarily. Deleting the app itself may leave local files behind. Email us with your app version and installation source for the appropriate removal steps. You do not need to send your record files.

Does the distance reminder record or upload my picture?

No. When it is on, the front camera only estimates your distance from the screen on the device during a break; nothing is recorded, stored or uploaded, and the camera turns off when the break ends. This estimate depends on the camera, device and individual differences. It is an approximate reminder, not a calibrated measurement. Turn it off in Adjust, or withdraw camera permission in system settings.

Is the clarity check a medical test?

No. It notes how things look to you on this screen today: how close and how far a line of small letters stays sharp, which side of a red/green split looks sharper, and which direction on a clock face looks darker, and compares that with the last check on the same screen. Screen, brightness, light and how you feel all change it. It says nothing about how your eyes are or whether they changed, and it does not replace an examination. If something keeps looking different, or feels uncomfortable, see a professional.

Does passing a challenge or moving up mean my eyes got better?

No. Levels and challenges only reflect what you could make out on this screen, in this image, and depend on screen size, brightness, distance and how you feel at the moment. They say nothing about any change in your eyes or any effect. IAEyes is not a medical product and does not diagnose, treat or improve eye conditions; if you are uncomfortable or have questions, consult a professional.
